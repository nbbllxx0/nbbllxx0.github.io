Trustworthy AI for engineering design: the AI proposes, the physics decides.

I build AI systems for mechanical design that an engineer can check. My work covers agents that operate engineering solvers, tests that show when an AI result can be believed, and methods that keep the engineer in the loop. It rests on generative models for architected materials and fast GPU solvers for topology optimization.

Research program

Making AI-assisted engineering trustworthy

AI now proposes mechanical designs faster than engineers can evaluate them. It also makes errors that look like success: a converged optimization of a misread problem, an answer to an impossible request, a speed-up measured against a weak baseline. My research asks when an AI-proposed design deserves to be believed, in three themes built on one physical foundation.

Agentic engineering

Language-model agents that configure, steer and repair engineering solvers, with every action accepted or rejected by a deterministic check.

Theme 01

AI system verification

Benchmarks, audits and acceptance tests that separate a correct AI result from one that only looks correct.

Theme 02

Human–AI collaboration

Systems that ask the engineer the question that matters, refuse with reasons, and return geometry the engineer can edit.

Theme 03

Physical foundations

Generative models for architected materials and open GPU solvers that make the checks affordable inside the design loop.

Foundation

Theme 01

Agentic engineering

Language models can now read a solver's state and act on it. I use them as a control layer: the model sets continuation parameters from the live solver state, configures a topology-optimization problem from a plain-English description, or proposes spatial edits after each solve. Deterministic checks decide what is accepted. Two open-source tools carry this into full design sessions: AgenticTO turns a written brief into a three-dimensional part, and AMM designs periodic cells for stiffness and elastic-wave band gaps. Each releases a design only when a solver-issued certificate passes.

Across three studies the model steers the solver competitively, but a well-designed deterministic baseline stays close or ahead each time. I report that result as it is. It is the reason acceptance rests with explicit checks and not with the model.

Theme 02

AI system verification

An AI result can report success and still be wrong. I build the tests that expose those cases, for language models, for learned design methods, and for the numerical solvers underneath them.

  • Impossible problems reported as solved. On paired valid and impossible mechanics problems, three recent language models missed 12 of 90 rejections. In 11 of those 12, the model stated the flaw, answered a corrected problem, and still reported the original as solved.
  • Speed claims depend on the baseline. Re-running released machine-learning topology-optimization codes on one workstation, an efficient implementation of the same classical algorithm cut two methods' speed-ups by up to 20-fold.
  • A fast estimate can accept a false claim. For periodic cells, a grid-aware checker accepted none of 750 false stiffness and solid-fraction claims near the threshold, while the finest-grid estimate alone accepted 42.
  • A converged solve is not always converged. On 4 of 102 held-out states, a multigrid solver's stopping residual passed while the true residual was 1.35 to 49.5 times the tolerance. A residual guard catches all four.

The same question, what does a learned transformation destroy?, drives related work on defect evidence in semiconductor inspection, risk certificates for industrial image restoration, and calibrated remaining-useful-life prediction under operating-regime shift.

Theme 03

Human–AI collaboration

The engineer should stay in control of what an AI system releases. I design three behaviours into the system for that purpose.

  • Ask. When a specification leaves loads or supports open, consequence-guided clarification releases a design only if its compliance stays within 5% of the reference at every reading still possible. Otherwise it asks the question that matters most for the design. In 26 of 36 tasks, the design for the default reading failed at another reading.
  • Refuse with reasons. A lattice search either returns a cell that a second solver can rebuild or refuses the request, naming the minimal set of conflicting constraints and the slack of a repair.
  • Stay editable. Generative models that output B-splines and NURBS let an engineer move a control point and see a local, predictable change.

Foundation

Generative geometry and fast solvers

Inverse design of architected materials. Given target effective properties, which cellular geometries achieve them? The answer is rarely unique, and that is useful: a manufacturing constraint can eliminate one candidate without collapsing the design space. Style-IH-GAN generates diverse triply periodic minimal surface unit cells for prescribed properties, with fidelity checked by numerical homogenization over 750 samples. CH-WGAN adds uncertainty-aware generation of three-dimensional aperiodic structures through signed distance fields.

Scalable topology optimization. Matrix-free 3D SIMP with fused Gather-GEMM-Scatter kernels and a matrix-free Galerkin multigrid solver put large three-dimensional topology optimization on a single GPU, released as open source under the BSD 3-Clause license. These solvers are what make verification affordable: a fast solve lets a check run inside the design loop.

The model proposes; the solver certifies

In the agent pipelines, no result is accepted on the model's own report. Acceptance is a deterministic, inspectable check.

Report the baseline honestly

The agent studies compare against strong deterministic baselines, and say so when the baseline wins.

Release the code

Code for solvers, pipelines and benchmarks is released under open licenses as the papers appear, so other groups can reproduce and reuse it.

Research roadmap

From verified simulation to evidence-carrying design agents

The long-term goal is an engineering agent that releases a design only together with the evidence for it. The plan below builds toward that goal in stages. Items after the first stage are planned directions, not completed work.

  1. Today

    Ph.D. record

    • Agent pipelines whose outputs pass deterministic acceptance checks
    • Benchmarks and audits for AI claims in mechanics and topology optimization
    • Open GPU solvers for three-dimensional topology optimization
  2. Years 1–2

    Evidence with every prediction

    • An open virtual-testing benchmark for graded lattices: homogenized predictions against full-resolution GPU finite-element analysis, with the error reported alongside each prediction
    • Clarification extended from loads and supports to material properties, manufacturing limits and measurement uncertainty
  3. Years 3–5

    From simulation to measurement

    • Component models updated from measured response, with calibrated uncertainty, in collaboration with groups that hold sensing and test data
    • Agents that decide which simulation or experiment to run next
    • Manufacturable designs with explicit acceptance tests
  4. Long term

    Evidence-carrying design agents

    • Design agents that release, ask or refuse, and attach the evidence for the decision
    • Open verification tools that other groups use to check AI-proposed designs

My record is computational. The sensing and experimental links are proposed collaborations, not existing capabilities.

Style-IH-GAN: Diverse Inverse Homogenization and Continuous Graded Synthesis of Cellular Metamaterials

S. Yang, J. Wang

Engineering with Computers 42:148, 2026 open access

Paper
Details

Inverse design of cellular metamaterials is one-to-many: many geometries realize the same target properties, but conditional generators tend to collapse onto one. Style-IH-GAN uses a style-modulated, property-conditioned generator with mode-seeking and property-consistency losses to produce genuinely different TPMS unit cells for a fixed target, and emits spatially graded fields in a single batched pass with trilinear interpolation for continuous inter-cell geometry.

  • 2.2× higher shape-parameter diversity and 19% lower surrogate Young’s-modulus error than a vanilla conditional GAN
  • Fidelity checked by numerical homogenization over 750 generated samples across 15 targets
  • A 300-cell graded field is generated in sub-millisecond time
  • Stated limitation: accuracy is strongest inside the training manifold; boundary and extrapolated-density targets need screening

CH-WGAN: Uncertainty-Aware Generative Design of Three-Dimensional Aperiodic Architected Cellular Materials via Signed Distance Fields

S. Yang, J. Wang

Journal of Mechanical Design 148(12):121701, 2026

Paper
Details

Aperiodic architected cellular materials trade directional performance for isotropy and robustness, but introduce geometric uncertainty that complicates validation and manufacturing. CH-WGAN learns how aperiodic variants depart from a periodic parent: a parameter generator maps nominal controls, an aperiodicity code and noise into a convex mixture of analytic signed distance functions whose scale and periodicity distributions model the uncertainty explicitly.

  • Conditional hierarchical Wasserstein GAN with an InfoGAN-style Q-head for invertibility and multimodal coverage
  • A periodicalization module warps the 3D grid according to learned periodicity distributions
  • Generates multiple structurally diverse aperiodic variants for one periodic parent; learned uncertainty matches real aperiodicity statistics

Large Language Models as Optimization Controllers: Adaptive Continuation for SIMP Topology Optimization

S. Yang, J. Wang, Y. Wang

Advances in Engineering Software 223:104304, 2026

PaperarXivCode
Details

A large language model acts as a state-conditioned control layer for SIMP topology optimization: every k-th iteration it reads a structured solver-state observation and sets the penalization exponent, projection sharpness, filter radius and move limit, instead of following a fixed open-loop continuation schedule. A hard grayness gate reduces premature sharpening.

  • In single-run 2-D comparisons, 5.9–7.4% lower final compliance than the no-tail fixed baseline, and better than the expert, three-field, schedule-only and grayness-rule controllers
  • A strong fixed-plus-tail control is slightly better (0.4–1.1%), so the paper frames the contribution as a control layer that can execute and help discover competitive strategies, not superior numerical performance
  • Ablation, gate, budget, replay and model-sensitivity checks for attribution and reproducibility

S. Yang, J. Wang

arXivCode
Details

An engineering solver can converge even when a language agent has misread the user's problem. arXivCode
Details

AMM (AgenticMetamaterials) is an open-source Python tool that searches repeating cubic cells for targets on stiffness, solid fraction and complete elastic-wave band gaps. Search is kept separate from acceptance: a separate checker allows for changes as the simulation grid gets finer and, for gaps, tests for modes at sampled wave directions and wavelengths, so it can miss modes between samples.

  • In 1,500 near-threshold stiffness and solid-fraction tests, the checker accepted 454 of 750 claims supported by the reference and none of the 750 false claims; the finest-grid estimate alone accepted 42 false claims.
  • In an independent finite-element comparison on ten new cube-and-bar cells, it accepted one of two true gap claims and left all 17 false claims undecided.
  • It failed the protocol's conservative-edge test once, placing one upper gap edge too high, though it left that claim undecided.

When a Positive SIMP Density Floor Is Not Enough: Solver Admissibility and Guarded Floor Selection in Matrix-Free 3D Topology Optimization

S. Yang, J. Wang, Y. Wang

arXiv:2607.26382, 2026 under review

arXivCode
Details

In a matrix-free multigrid FGMRES solver for 3D SIMP, a reported converged solve is not always converged: the projected residual used for stopping can fall below tolerance while the true residual is far above it. The paper treats density-floor selection as a verified control problem and accepts no solution until the true relative residual is recomputed and meets 1e-6.

  • On 4 of 102 held-out states the stopping residual passes while the true residual is 1.35–49.5× the tolerance
  • A two-feature rule matches 98 of 102 reference classifications; the residual guard catches the other four, so all 102 selected solves meet the tolerance
  • Preserves the original operator on 24 admissible states, at 2.5× the mean wall time of always using a 1e-3 floor

Consequence-Guided Clarification: Releasing Minimum-Compliance Topology-Optimized Designs from Incomplete Specifications

S. Yang, J. Wang

arXiv:2610.34957, 2026 preprint

arXiv
Details

When loads, supports or the domain are underspecified, a converged topology optimization can solve the wrong problem. Consequence-guided clarification computes a reference design for each complete reading of the specification and releases a design only if its compliance is within 5% of the reference at every reading still consistent with the answers; otherwise it asks the question whose worst answer leaves the smallest worst-case excess.

  • Evaluated on 36 arXiv-derived tasks, treating each of 134 readings in turn as the intended one.
  • In 26 tasks, the design for the default reading failed at another reading.
  • Outside always-asked questions, asking about everything took 188 questions; both variants took 105.
  • References are not certified optima: better ones pushed three accepted cases above tolerance, and finer meshes changed some decisions.

Teaching and service

Verification as a primary skill

A student leaving my course should be able to take an engineering question, choose a computational approach, implement it, and then explain why the answer deserves to be believed. Software will produce a stress contour or an optimized topology; it will not say whether the result is right. So I teach verification as a primary skill: predict before you compute, check against known solutions, and treat disagreement between intuition and simulation as the lesson.
